# Reader examples

These tie-breaks were written before the new replies were available. They elaborate the [frozen rubric](plan.md).

- All three stimuli say that A kept thinking about the moment. Treat “it stuck with you” and “it left a strong impression” as ordinary paraphrases here. A comparison with what A expected, knew, or realized adds a proposition.
- In a named-feeling condition, asking what about the moment made A feel that way can invite an open account. Distinguish this from supplying a particular cause such as being ridiculed, feeling included, or wanting approval. A's words do not specify whom the laughter targeted.
- “That makes sense”, an offer to listen, or B describing their own feeling can leave A's account open. An assertion that the scene must have been painful or was a sign of belonging adds a reading of the event.
- A yes/no candidate can receive OFFER when it introduces a specific interpretation. A question beginning with “what” or “why” can receive ASSUME when understanding the question requires accepting an unreported premise. Apply the code to the whole line, including both sides of a dash.
- Preserve UNCLEAR for a material ambiguity in the wording. Record short evidence notes and retain a first judgment even if a repeated line later receives a different judgment.
